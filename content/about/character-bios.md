# The People — Ethan & Jacob

*Yes, we wrote each other's. No, we didn't let each other edit first.*

---

## Ethan Highfield
*Vermont. Triplet. Talks to strangers. Cries at scenery.*
**— written by Jacob**

Ethan grew up on a quiet road outside Burlington, Vermont, one of three — his brother Ben is his identical twin, and his sister Abby is older by eight minutes and in charge of all of them. He has never once been alone, even inside his own head, and somehow he still turned out to be the most open person I've ever met. I mean, it took a while, ya know. But it happened.

Here's the short version: he feels everything, all the way down, out loud. Well, now it's out loud. It didn't use to be. He'll cry at a canyon, narrate a sunrise like a nature documentary, and talk to the guy at the next gas pump like they've been friends for years. When I met him, he was holding himself so tight he could barely breathe, terrified of being seen. Watching him stop being afraid of that has been the best thing I've ever gotten to witness.

He packs four jackets for every trip and wears none of them — well, except that gray hoodie. He's brave in the ways that actually count. He's the reason I started wanting things again.

That's Ethan. You'll like him. Everybody does.

**— J**

---

## Jacob Monroe
*Minnesota. Senator's kid. Quiet. Steady. Annoyingly always right.*
**— written by Ethan**

Okay so. Jacob Monroe. Where do I even start.

He's from Minnesota. His dad's a state senator, and his mom's a rockstar who owns her own marketing company. A fact Jacob mentions approximately never. He came out to Vermont to stay with his Aunt Christine, which is the single luckiest thing that has ever happened to me. He's the quiet one. He says about a third of what he's actually thinking, and the other two-thirds you have to learn to read, and learning to read him is basically my favorite hobby.

Here's the thing about Jacob: he's been through some things that would've made most people close up for good. For a while, they did. He'd decided it was safer not to want anybody. And then, I still totally don't know how, he let me in. He notices everything. He gives me his jacket before I ask. And sometimes he checks the tire pressure nine times because he's trying not to say goodbye out loud.

He cannot dance. At all. It's genuinely tragic. I love him so much it's embarrassing.

That's my Jacob. I got really, really lucky.

**— E**
